How an industrial design reaches production

Follow a real embedded product from prototype through carrier design, qualification, factory programming and support. Identify what the customer still has to do.

What you should be able to explain

  • Explain why “the prototype worked” and “the customer is in production” require different evidence. Walk through one application and identify every party, deliverable and unresolved cost or timing question between those events.

Where the remaining evidence comes from

Public sources

  • Datasheet migration appendix; public certification/support programs; finished-system documentation; application-engineering interview.

Your terminal

  • Collect published customer case studies and historical management Q&A about project duration or conversion; a terminal cannot supply a specific customer’s unpublished qualification record.

Your conversations and observations

  • Ask an embedded product manager to narrate one completed project from requirements to production, using calendar milestones rather than a general satisfaction score.
  • Ask a design partner about a design that was revised or abandoned and what would have changed the choice.
  • At a lab, have an engineer inspect the carrier/module interface with you.

Evidence to have before moving on

  • One end-to-end project map with stage owners and exit documents.
  • A carrier/module responsibility matrix.
  • A completed-product parts-and-functions map plus a list of evidence still needed from an engineer.
