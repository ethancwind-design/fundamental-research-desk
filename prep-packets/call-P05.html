Opening questions and follow-up branches

1. Does the quantity shown for this exact SKU refer to stock available to this destination from your own warehouse today?

If yesDoes it include allocated, supplier-held or transfer stock? What does the delivery estimate promise and what can still change?
If noWhat does it include instead, and is there a separate public field for local shippable stock?
If vaguePlease explain this screenshot’s labels one by one; should I record the quantity as displayed availability rather than inventory owned?
Public evidenceA public stock-status glossary, ordering FAQ or corrected interpretation of a redacted screenshot.
PurposeThe panel needs consistent definitions before any observation can be compared.
Your queue

2. Can a small buyer arrange predictable repeated deliveries using an already published process for this module?

If yesWhere are minimum quantities, schedule changes, cancellation conditions and lead-time qualifications stated?
If noAt what point would the buyer need a sales discussion, and what facts should a genuine buyer prepare?
If vagueExplain the process for a hypothetical monthly requirement without quoting a volume discount, reserving stock or treating me as an order prospect.
Public evidencePublic scheduled-order policy, lifecycle notice route or an illustrative process checklist.
PurposeOrdering process can be researched honestly without creating fictitious purchase intent.
Your queue

3. Would a fall in the displayed stock count reliably mean customer purchases occurred?

If yesWhat other public mechanisms can move it, and which would invalidate a sales interpretation?
If noWhich field changes are ambiguous because of replenishment, transfers, allocation or web updates?
If vagueHere are two observations a few days apart. List the possible explanations we cannot distinguish from the page.
Public evidencePublic availability definitions and, if available, a generic explanation of stock-update timing; no actual customer orders.
PurposeThis explicitly blocks an unsupported estimate of sell-through from web snapshots.
Your queue

What to ask to see—and what this person cannot establish

Evidence requests

  • Meaning of each displayed quantity, delivery and warehouse field, with source/date.
  • Published lifecycle, product-change and returns notifications for one exact product.
  • SKU matching sheet showing manufacturer number, distributor number, RAM/storage/wireless variant and packaging.

Limits of this contact

  • A product specialist may know only one region, channel or catalogue view.
  • Distributor stock is not final-user consumption or Raspberry Pi’s group inventory.
  • A US help agent should not be assumed to know the UK licensee contract or unpublished manufacturer allocations.

Earn the next conversation

Contact 1Bring a small cross-site terminology sheet and say you want to avoid misreading their public catalogue in student stock research.
Next-contact trigger: Two completed observations with exact URLs.
Contact 2Return the corrected field definitions and one real catalogue ambiguity, with no claim about sales velocity.
Next-contact trigger: After applying their explanation to another scheduled observation.
Contact 3Share a clean glossary usable by first-time buyers and ask about an already published process change.
Next-contact trigger: Only when a listing or public policy materially changes.

Recruit a balanced sample

Interview one person at each of two channels separately; do not use one employee’s answer to define the other site. Include both in-stock and unavailable SKUs. Record refusals and missing fields. Keep PiShop’s kit/service perspective separate in P06.
