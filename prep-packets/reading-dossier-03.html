Why a board sale and a royalty look different in the accounts

Reconstruct the commercial and accounting routes before comparing revenue, units, gross profit or reported ASP.

What you should be able to explain

  • Using a hypothetical identical board, explain how reported revenue differs across the two routes without inserting an assumed margin. Then explain why the published ASP is not a substitute for realized group revenue per unit.

Definitions to keep separate

Direct distribution
The group procures or manufactures products and sells them through resellers or directly to OEMs; the report distinguishes this from licence royalties.Physical pp62–64, 88–90 · p62 ↗ (physical PDF page 62)
Reported board ASP
The KPI is a weighted manufacturer-recommended retail price for boards sold during the period, rather than group revenue divided by board units.Physical p33, KPI definitions · p33 ↗ (physical PDF page 33)
Gross profit per board
A defined board metric whose scope includes royalties; use the company’s numerator and denominator rather than an improvised revenue margin.Physical p33, KPI definitions · p33 ↗ (physical PDF page 33)

Where the remaining evidence comes from

Public sources

  • Prospectus licence summary; annual recognition policy, revenue tables and KPI definitions; subsequent RNS.

Your terminal

  • Obtain full results-call Q&A and broker definition notes to see whether channel changes or licence renewal were discussed; distinguish analyst estimates from company disclosure.

Your conversations and observations

  • Ask finance/IR for a public illustrative invoice-to-recognition example in each route.
  • Ask a distributor to explain its publicly discussable ordering and reporting process; do not request another customer’s transaction details.

Evidence to have before moving on

  • Goods/cash/accounting flow diagrams for both routes.
  • A KPI dictionary reproducing scope and units in your own words.
  • A blank-cell reconciliation showing every unavailable split rather than estimating it.
