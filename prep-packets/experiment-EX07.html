Observe an operator task behind a published deployment

Which steps, assistance and recovery responsibilities are visible in actual use, and which remain hidden from the operator?

Define the test before you start

Operator task: With permission, observe one ordinary non-sensitive task from initiation to a verifiable outcome during a quiet period. If a live workflow cannot be observed, ask the operator to demonstrate with dummy data.

Success rule: One fully described task with verified platform scope, elapsed/active/waiting steps and clear unknowns; no unsupported “time saved” comparison.

Materials

  • A printed task map from one dated case.
  • A willing current operator or a public authorised demonstration; no access obtained.
  • Notebook; no customer identities, payment data or workplace network access.

60 minutes preparation; 30–60 minutes on site if agreed; travel/fare only after a real location and appointment are confirmed.

Repeatable procedure

Step 1

DoConfirm the business, current product generation and how platform identification is known. If hardware identity cannot be verified, label the observation vendor-workflow only.
RecordIdentification evidence; date; role; historical versus current.

Step 2

DoAsk permission for observation and note-taking; agree which activities are out of scope. Do not photograph customers or internal systems.
RecordAllowed task; permitted evidence; attribution.

Step 3

DoObserve three ordinary task repetitions if operations permit; otherwise record the actual count. Timestamp user action, visible response, waiting and assistance.
RecordTask-step log; actual repetitions; manual timing precision.

Step 4

DoAsk about one ordinary failure/recovery process without causing a failure or interrupting live work.
RecordKnown incident versus hypothetical account; responsible person; cause known/unknown.

Step 5

DoVerify the task map with the operator; ask the technical owner separately what the operator cannot know.
RecordAgreement/disagreement; source type; remaining architecture question.

Controls, stopping rules and limits

Hold these conditions fixed

  • No intervention in live equipment, no enclosure opening and no scanning of the business network.
  • Keep supplier-selected venue, independent recruitment and public demo separately labelled.
  • Do not infer Pi presence from a touchscreen or public branding alone.

Stop when

  • Stop immediately if the operator is busy or permission changes.
  • If access fails, complete the dated source worksheet and mark observation not performed.

What this cannot establish

  • A short task observation does not establish labour savings, uptime or customer throughput.
  • A historical case study is promotional selection; current verification is required.
