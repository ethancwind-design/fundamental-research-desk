Opening questions and follow-up branches

1. Did you personally choose or reject the computing platform for this specific project?

If yesWhat was the task, stage, approximate decision date and your responsibility? Which alternatives made the actual shortlist?
If noWhich part did you personally build or use, and who made the platform decision? Keep your evidence limited to that part.
If vagueTell me the last concrete thing the device had to do; a general opinion about a brand will not fill this row.
Public evidencePublic repository, published project description, photo of owned hardware or a detailed nonconfidential process account.
PurposeRole and a real project establish whether the interview is first-hand.
Your queue

2. Was one testable requirement decisive in keeping, abandoning or avoiding Pi?

If yesWhat was the pass/fail threshold and which available option met it? Was that measured then or remembered now?
If noWas the decision driven by familiarity, availability, an existing application or a bundle of constraints? Rank only the factors you actually used.
If vagueIf price had been identical, would the same decision have followed? Then test the same counterfactual for software familiarity and delivered availability.
Public evidenceContemporaneous public notes or a repeatable small test; clearly label retrospective counterfactual answers as recollection.
PurposeDifferent rejection mechanisms require different source checks.
Your queue

3. Would a change to one publicly observable product feature have changed the decision at that time?

If yesSpecify the feature, required level and remaining blockers. Is it available now, or is this purely hypothetical?
If noWhat makes the decision insensitive to hardware changes—for example a mandated application or support process?
If vagueChoose one factor from the previous answer and explain the chain from specification to completed task.
Public evidenceA source-linked requirement table; no prediction of future purchases.
PurposeThis separates replaceable technical friction from circumstances outside the platform.

What to ask to see—and what this person cannot establish

Evidence requests

  • Same seven-field project chronology for all six people.
  • One corroborating artefact or explicit “no artefact available” for each account.
  • Respondent-reported commercial affiliation, Pi familiarity, project stage and recruitment route.

Limits of this contact

  • Six purposively selected accounts cannot estimate market share, customer churn or population satisfaction.
  • Abandoning a project is not necessarily rejecting its hardware.
  • Hindsight and social desirability can rewrite the stated decision; retain the date and evidence type.

Earn the next conversation

Contact 1Invite participation in a student stock-research methods exercise; offer the reusable decision worksheet, regardless of platform or view.
Next-contact trigger: Host permits the invitation and the participant volunteers.
Contact 2Return only their own factual chronology for optional correction; do not ask them to endorse your interpretation.
Next-contact trigger: Within a week of a substantive interview.
Contact 3Share an anonymised methods summary and relevant public technical resources only if they opted in; show unresolved disagreements.
Next-contact trigger: After the sample closes, not repeated requests for anecdotes.

Recruit a balanced sample

Target six, with two in each category and at least two recruitment pools. Cap any one referral chain at two people. Use the first eligible consenting respondents in each category rather than selecting the strongest stories. Record approach/decline/eligibility counts without private identifiers. If a category is unfilled, report the gap instead of relabelling a current user.
