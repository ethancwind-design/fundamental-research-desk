Opening questions and follow-up branches

1. Does the system in this dated public story still describe a workflow you personally use or maintain?

If yesWhich hardware generation, software responsibility and operator task remain the same, and which have changed publicly?
If noWas the task retired, redesigned or moved to another platform? Which parts of that change may you discuss publicly?
If vagueLet us label every box: personally verified today, historical article only, or unknown. Who could verify the unknown boxes without disclosing private operations?
Public evidenceCurrent public manual, public talk, permitted demonstration or participant’s own nonconfidential process description.
PurposeA historical deployment is an entry point, not evidence of an unchanged installed base.
Your queue

2. When the system stops being useful, can an ordinary operator restore service without its original developer?

If yesPlease demonstrate or describe the actual approved runbook and separate waiting time from hands-on recovery.
If noWhich step requires a specialist or physical access, and what work continues during the interruption?
If vagueTake the last ordinary incident you are allowed to discuss. Was the cause known, or only the symptom? Avoid guessing that the Pi board failed.
Public evidenceBlank runbook, public support policy or a synthetic demo; no live incident logs, private production metrics or customer data.
PurposeMaintenance depends on people and process as well as the underlying hardware.
Your queue

3. Does the person using the system also influence the choice to upgrade, replace or keep it?

If yesWhat evidence do they consider: compatibility, serviceability, task performance or acquisition cost? Give a public or hypothetical example.
If noWhich role owns that decision, and could you offer an introduction only if they are willing?
If vagueSeparate user preference, technical approval and purchasing authority on this decision map.
Public evidenceRole map and public procurement/technical criteria; no unpublished budgets or order intentions.
PurposeA satisfied operator and a hardware purchasing decision are different evidence.

What to ask to see—and what this person cannot establish

Evidence requests

  • Three-column case-study ledger: source assertion/date, current first-hand observation, unresolved status.
  • Task sequence with touchpoints, waiting, assistance and recovery, if observation is permitted.
  • Permissioned notes on who owns the application, device maintenance and hardware choice.

Limits of this contact

  • Operators may not know the embedded processor, supplier economics or purchase contract.
  • A visible touch screen does not prove that it contains Raspberry Pi.
  • A supplier-referred venue is selected evidence; an edited case study cannot establish typical uptime or labour savings.
