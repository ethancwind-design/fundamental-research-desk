Opening questions and follow-up branches

1. Would a bare Compute Module satisfy the complete requirements in this read-only monitoring example?

If yesIdentify the exact module, interfaces and environmental envelope; which finished-system obligations still sit with your product?
If noWhich added hardware, software, enclosure or testing makes the difference? Prioritise the first blocking requirement.
If vaguePlease annotate the requirements sheet: fulfilled by module, fulfilled by your product, or still the customer’s job.
Public evidencePublished finished-product datasheet, installation manual, certifications and a public system diagram.
PurposeThe reader needs to understand what an industrial integrator adds beyond buying a module.
Your queue

2. Can an existing customer migrate to a newer Compute Module without repeating qualification or changing field maintenance?

If yesWhich published configurations are covered, and how was compatibility validated?
If noWhich gate tends to require new work: I/O, thermal design, software image, certification or support policy?
If vagueDescribe one public released-product transition by milestones; no customer names, pricing terms or unreleased design wins needed.
Public evidencePublic migration note, released manual revisions and an illustrative acceptance checklist.
PurposeA product refresh is not automatically a replacement sale or frictionless design-in.
Your queue

3. Is there an application in your published range for which you would recommend a different processor or platform?

If yesWhich concrete constraint changes the recommendation, and what public specification shows the boundary?
If noWhat are the outer limits of this Pi-based product’s published operating envelope?
If vagueChoose one constraint from my sheet—deterministic timing, temperature, certified function or Windows-only software—and explain the decision method.
Public evidenceTwo public product specifications matched to one requirements sheet; no confidential bid outcome.
PurposeCounterexamples prevent an approved partner’s success story from becoming a universal claim.
Your queue

What to ask to see—and what this person cannot establish

Evidence requests

  • Current finished-product datasheet and support/lifecycle terms, dated and versioned.
  • A filled requirements-responsibility matrix for an explicitly hypothetical non-safety-critical logger.
  • One public released-product transition and one contrasting platform specification.

Limits of this contact

  • A sales engineer may favour products they sell and may not know an end customer’s realised economics.
  • A generic requirement discussion is not certification, paid engineering advice or a quotation.
  • Design Partner status, product manufacture and customer use are different relationships; verify each separately.

Earn the next conversation
