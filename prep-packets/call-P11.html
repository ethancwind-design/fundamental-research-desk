Opening questions and follow-up branches

1. Have you personally used Pi or a competing platform for a project with a clear working end state?

If yesWhat was that task and what did you do yourself? Would you be comfortable showing a public or nonconfidential artefact?
If noWould a short novice-usability attempt interest you, or should I speak with someone who volunteers for that?
If vagueLet us choose one finished or abandoned project rather than a general favourite board.
Public evidencePublic project artefact or participant-approved notes, without personal credentials or employer information.
PurposeThe aim is a grounded workflow account, not recruitment of enthusiasts by sentiment.
Your queue

2. Could you follow this one-page setup or recovery step without my verbal help?

If yesPlease try from the stated start condition, saying where you need to interpret an instruction; I will record interventions, not score your ability.
If noWhich prerequisite is missing—equipment, permissions, prior knowledge or the written instruction?
If vagueChoose one step that blocks progress and say what information would make it executable.
Public evidenceTiming and step/intervention log from an agreed harmless task; no unattended access to the participant’s device.
PurposeAnother operator reveals hidden assumptions in the researcher’s own successful build.
Your queue

3. Would another platform have met this same project requirement with less work for you?

If yesWhich actual difference matters and what part can we reproduce or verify in a public source?
If noWhat existing knowledge, library, interface or support process keeps the chosen platform suitable?
If vagueName the one requirement that cannot be relaxed; compare platforms only on that requirement first.
Public evidenceParticipant’s requirement sheet and a matching official alternative-platform document, to be sourced if selected.
PurposeFamiliarity and a true product constraint must not be conflated.
Your queue

What to ask to see—and what this person cannot establish

Evidence requests

  • Anonymous task-trial sheet including prior experience, exact start state and every intervention.
  • One project-choice history, allowing current, lapsed and alternative users.
  • A corrected runbook that helps the participant or space without claiming their endorsement.

Limits of this contact

  • Makerspace visitors do not represent industrial purchasing or all Pi users.
  • Available tools, staff or a visitor’s device are not automatically available for your test.
  • Two runs on one device cannot estimate reliability; a novice’s error does not by itself identify a product defect.

Earn the next conversation

Contact 1Ask the host for a permitted short voluntary activity and offer the resulting neutral checklist to the space.
Next-contact trigger: Host confirms the date, rules and relevance.
Contact 2Return the actual corrected runbook and thank participants for specific documentation fixes; do not add them to any mailing list.
Next-contact trigger: After an attended session and only through agreed channels.
Contact 3Offer to share a short demonstration of your own measured results at a public event if wanted, with failures and limitations intact.
Next-contact trigger: Only after a real result; do not use volunteering as a pretext for private introductions.

Recruit a balanced sample

Ask the host to share a neutral criterion-based invitation, then accept the first eligible volunteers. Record skill level and Pi relationship before the test. Seek at least one alternative/lapsed user through a second pool; do not ask the host to identify private members.
