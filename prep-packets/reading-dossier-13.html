Who approves spending, and what the incentives measure

Read decision authority, pay measures, capital commitments and shareholder approvals as separate records. Build an evidence file for management discussions without judging alignment on their behalf.

What you should be able to explain

  • Explain one historical capital decision by separating decision-maker, stated criteria, cash spent, remaining commitment and shareholder authority. Describe the relevant incentive measures factually without concluding whether they are good or bad.

Definitions to keep separate

Annual bonus versus LTIP
The remuneration report uses different measures, periods, vesting and holding arrangements for annual incentives and long-term awards.Physical pp72–75 · p72 ↗ (physical PDF page 72)
Authority versus action

Where the remaining evidence comes from

Public sources

  • Board schedule, remuneration report, annual cash/commitment disclosures, AGM votes and executive-change RNS.

Your terminal

  • Cross-check terminal ownership and share-count summaries against the hosted primary-source RNS reading copy, retaining timestamps. Retrieve historical management Q&A on capital priorities rather than downloading the same 82 announcements again.

Your conversations and observations

  • Ask IR or the company secretary which public policy governs a named allocation decision.
  • Ask management for a completed decision example and the criteria it applied, using information it can discuss publicly.
  • At an AGM, ask a focused question that refers to a specific remuneration definition or approved authority.

Evidence to have before moving on

  • An approval-authority matrix.
  • An incentive-measure and award-status ledger.
  • A cash/commitment/authority record with dates and public follow-through evidence.
