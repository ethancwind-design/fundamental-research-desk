Revision, kit contents and provenance check

Can the owner match the physical device and kit to its documented variant and support route?

Define the test before you start

Operator task: Create one device identity card per unit and match board markings, reported model/revision, kit contents, seller part number and published support route.

Success rule: Every claimed match cites a source; an unfamiliar revision remains unknown rather than labelled counterfeit or defective.

Materials

  • Two or more owned/permissioned devices or kits if available, original invoices with personal details redacted, camera.
  • Official documentation for each exact board and seller listing; no suspected-counterfeit purchase.

1–2 hours; no device purchase or destructive inspection.

RecordIncluded/missing/substituted; source URL/date.

Step 4

DoAsk the relevant reseller about any real discrepancy using the public contact route, with an honest research purpose and a redacted record.
RecordExplanation; official notice; still unknown.

Step 5

DoCompare the two identity cards only for compatibility and disclosed differences; keep sample-size limits explicit.
RecordRevision difference; documentation coverage; support path.

Controls, stopping rules and limits

Hold these conditions fixed

  • Only inspect owned/borrowed devices with permission; do not open sealed store products.
  • Visual differences and revision codes are not alone proof of authenticity.
  • Do not expose personal invoices, network identifiers or unique device serials in a public deliverable.

Stop when

  • If disassembly would void permission or disturb a working system, use exterior labels and public records only.
  • Ask the owner before any command or photograph.

What this cannot establish

  • A few samples cannot establish counterfeit prevalence, manufacturing yield or regional production mix.
  • Revision lists may not be exhaustive; preserve the documented limitation.
