What is actually inside a Raspberry Pi product?

Learn the board, module and chip boundaries well enough to inspect a product, read a customer design and identify whose technology performs each function.

What you should be able to explain

  • Explain a CM5-based finished product to another analyst using a blank sheet: identify the application processor, peripheral controller, memory, carrier, software and supplier for each element. Do not rely on “Raspberry Pi makes the whole computer” as the explanation.

Definitions to keep separate

SBC versus compute module
A single-board computer exposes usable connectors; a compute module packages the core computing functions for integration with a carrier and other system components.Physical pp57, 80 · p57 ↗ (physical PDF page 57)
RP1
Raspberry Pi’s peripheral controller used alongside the main application processor in Pi 5; it is not a replacement name for Broadcom BCM2712.Physical pp5–7 · p5 ↗ (physical PDF page 5)
Microcontroller
RP2040/RP2350 belongs to a separate device category from the Linux application processor in a Pi board.Physical pp57, 82 · p57 ↗ (physical PDF page 57)

Where the remaining evidence comes from

Public sources

  • Product briefs, datasheets, architecture interviews and report diagrams.

Your terminal

  • Use terminal product/segment labels only after translating them to the company’s disclosed product categories; no terminal is needed for circuit architecture.

Your conversations and observations

  • Ask an embedded engineer to check the diagrams and identify anything a first-time integrator has omitted.
  • At a maker or engineering lab, inspect an SBC, compute module, carrier and MCU board side by side.

Evidence to have before moving on

  • Two annotated architecture diagrams: Pi 5/CM5 and an RP2350-based application.
  • A component-responsibility matrix with source locators.
  • A product vocabulary sheet written without promotional adjectives.
