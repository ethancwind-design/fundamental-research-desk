Opening questions and follow-up branches

1. For a carrier built for CM4, does sharing the CM5 form factor remove all redesign and recertification work?

If yesPlease identify the exact configuration and current compatibility document that supports that answer; are power, connectors, interfaces and enclosure tested too?
If noWhich interfaces should be checked first, and what bench evidence would demonstrate that the migration is complete?
If vagueTake my proposed local kiosk: which claim can be checked from the datasheet and which requires an actual measurement?
Public evidenceCurrent datasheet sections, a published migration note, reference-carrier design or public test checklist.
PurposeMechanical interchangeability and finished-system compatibility are different engineering questions.
Your queue

2. Is there a published RP1 example that exposes a practical trade-off an integrator should test rather than assume away?

If yesChoose one ordinary peripheral or driver scenario and identify the versioned example I can reproduce on my own equipment.
If noWhat was the intended boundary between the RP1 design and the host processor that is already described publicly?
If vagueCan we label the blocks in my diagram: who supplies silicon, who owns firmware, and which public interface specification governs each boundary?
Public evidencePublic RP1 documentation, interview segment, design example or a released driver reference.
PurposeThe interview becomes a testable engineering workflow, not a general claim about proprietary silicon.

What to ask to see—and what this person cannot establish

Evidence requests

  • Annotated public block diagram with questions routed to Pi, component supplier and integrator.
  • Datasheet version/date, publicly available compliance document and the precise product covered.
  • A public example workload or failure reproduction that can be repeated without privileged tools.

Limits of this contact

  • A hardware engineer cannot tell you customer purchasing intentions or competitor-wide design-win rates.
  • A prototype demonstration cannot establish production yield, certification or years of reliability.
  • Do not seek unreleased product specifications or confidential supplier price schedules.
